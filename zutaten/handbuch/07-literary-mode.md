# Module 07 — Literary mode

Everything in Modules 01–06 still applies. This module describes what changes in
emphasis when the point of the book is not the resolution of a plot.

The distinction is not quality. There is excellent genre fiction and dull
literary fiction. The distinction is **where the book puts its weight**.

| | Genre mode | Literary mode |
|---|---|---|
| Organising question | What happens next? | What does this mean? |
| Reader's reward | Resolution | Recognition |
| Prose | Transparent, serving the story | Part of the content itself |
| Structure | Delivers the contract | May be the argument |
| Ending | Answers | Resonates, may withhold |
| Character | Changes to solve the problem | Is revealed; may fail to change |
| Pace | Forward | Deep |

---

## 1. What replaces plot pressure

Literary fiction still needs drive, but it can come from sources other than
"will they succeed?":

- **Curiosity about a person.** A voice interesting enough that the reader wants
  to keep listening. This is the most common literary engine.
- **Dread.** A structure that makes the reader certain something is coming.
- **Retrospective tension.** The narrator tells us early that it ended badly;
  the tension is in *how*.
- **Moral tension.** The reader watching a character approach a choice they will
  have to judge.
- **Formal curiosity.** The reader wanting to see how the pattern completes.

The failure mode of literary fiction is a book where none of these is present
and the writer has assumed good sentences are sufficient. They are not. A
literary novel still has to make the reader need the next page.

---

## 2. Voice as primary content

In literary mode, voice is not a delivery mechanism. It carries the meaning.

Build it from:

- **A characteristic angle of attention.** What this narrator finds worth
  noticing, and what they walk past.
- **A characteristic syntax.** Does thought accumulate in long subordinated
  chains, or arrive in flat declaratives?
- **A relationship to the reader.** Confiding, evasive, performing, indifferent.
- **A gap between what is said and what is known.** The most productive literary
  voice is one the reader can see past.

Free indirect discourse (Module 02) is the central technique. The narration
takes on the character's idiom and judgements while staying in third person,
which lets the prose be simultaneously inside the character and above them.

---

## 3. Theme without preaching

Theme is not a message. It is a **question the book examines honestly**.

State it as a tension, not a moral:

- Weak (a moral): "Loyalty matters more than ambition."
- Working (a question): "What does loyalty cost when it is not returned?"

### Techniques

**Build it as an argument with credible opposition.** Give at least two
characters a genuinely defensible position on the central question, each grounded
in different experience, each of which solves a real problem before its limits
show under pressure. A theme where only the protagonist is right is propaganda.

**Track who pays.** For every significant decision, note three parties: who
chose, who benefits, who bears the cost. When these separate, the story acquires
moral weight automatically.

**Repeat the pressure at different scales.** Let the same underlying question
appear as a small private choice, then again as an institutional one with
irreversible stakes. Readers compare rather than merely remember.

**Let objects carry it.** A recurring physical thing — a repaired object, a
photograph, a boundary line — accumulates meaning through how different
characters treat it. Motifs work because they are literal first and meaningful
second.

### The deletion test

If the theme disappears when you delete one explanatory paragraph, the story has
not made its case. The pattern of action, consequence and choice must carry it
without that paragraph. Then delete the paragraph anyway.

### What never to do

Do not have a character articulate the theme accurately at the end. Do not have
the narrator explain what an image meant. Do not resolve the thematic question
neatly; the reader should leave still holding it.

## 4. Structure in literary mode

**Kishōtenketsu** (Module 01) is often a better fit than three-act, because the
turn is a shift in understanding rather than a reversal of fortune.

Other viable shapes:

- **Accretive.** Scenes that do not cause each other, arranged so that the
  meaning accumulates. Requires strong motivic control to avoid feeling random.
- **Circular.** The book returns to its opening situation, which now reads
  differently.
- **Braided.** Two timelines or two lives, converging thematically rather than
  causally.
- **Fragmentary.** Deliberate gaps the reader fills. The gaps must be *shaped*;
  arbitrary omission reads as evasion.

Whatever the shape, the scene-level rules from Module 05 still hold. Literary
permission is not permission for scenes that do not turn.

---

## 5. Ambiguity — used correctly

Ambiguity is a tool with one legitimate use: when two readings are both fully
supported by the text and the tension between them is the point.

It is being misused when:

- The writer has not decided what happened
- Precision would require work that has not been done
- The ending is unresolved because no ending was found

Test: can you state both readings and point to the evidence for each? If not, it
is vagueness wearing ambiguity's coat.

---

## 6. Prose in literary mode

Higher tolerance for: long sentences, unusual syntax, digression, sustained
description, sentences whose pleasure is their own.

**No tolerance at all for:** vagueness, abstraction where a concrete noun would
serve, and decoration. Literary prose is *more* precise than genre prose, not
more ornamental. The most common failure of AI-written literary fiction is
mistaking density of abstraction for depth.

Test every unusual sentence: is it doing something a plain sentence could not?
If not, make it plain.

**Purple prose** is metaphor that draws attention to the writer rather than the
subject, adjective stacks, and abstraction where an image was needed. It is a
particular risk here because the mode permits ambition. The correction is
concreteness, not restraint.

---

## 7. Endings

Literary endings are permitted to withhold resolution, but they must deliver
**arrival** — a sense that the book has reached the place it was going.

Working closes:

- An image that gathers the book's motifs without explaining them
- A small action that shows the change (or the refusal to change)
- A shift in the narrator's relationship to their own story
- A return to the opening situation with everything now differently weighted

Not working: stopping. A book that ends because the material ran out is felt as
such immediately.

---

## Literary mode audit

- [ ] Can I state the thematic question as a question with two defensible answers?
- [ ] Is there a character who holds the opposing position well?
- [ ] Would the theme survive deleting every sentence that states it?
- [ ] Is there a reason to turn the page that is not plot? Name it.
- [ ] Does the voice have a consistent angle of attention?
- [ ] Take five ambitious sentences: does each do work a plain sentence could not?
- [ ] Is any ambiguity supported by two readings I can both evidence?
- [ ] Does the final page arrive, or merely stop?
