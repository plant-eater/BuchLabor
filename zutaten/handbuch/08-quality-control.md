# Module 08 — Quality control

Run this module as a **separate pass, in a fresh context**, so the model reading
the draft is not the model defending it. Self-critique inside the same context as
the drafting almost always produces agreement with the draft.

---

## 1. The story bible

Not part of the craft, but the book breaks without it. Maintain one file,
updated after every chapter, consulted before every scene.

```
CHARACTERS
  name | age | appearance (3 fixed facts) | speech markers | what they know, as of ch. X
TIMELINE
  chapter | day/date | elapsed time | location
FACTS
  established details that cannot change (geography, rules, relationships, objects)
PROMISES
  page N: [question raised] → answered on page M / OPEN
THREADS
  subplot | last touched in ch. X | current state
```

The **PROMISES** table is the most important. Every question raised must be
answered or deliberately left open, and a model writing a long book will
otherwise forget three-quarters of them.

---

## 2. Pass structure

Do not attempt to fix everything at once. Six passes, in this order, because
each one can invalidate the work of the passes below it.

### Pass 1 — Structural
*Working at chapter level. Prose is irrelevant here.*

- Does the 25% hinge exist and is it a choice by the protagonist?
- Does the midpoint reverse something?
- Is the 75% low point a defeat of the protagonist's *method*?
- Does the climax require the protagonist to act on what they learned?
- Chapter-by-chapter: delete each in turn on paper. Which deletions cost nothing?
- Is there a stretch of three or more chapters with no irreversible change?

Fix before proceeding. Prose polish on a chapter that will be cut is wasted work.

### Pass 2 — Character
- Ghost / Lie / Want / Need stated for protagonist and antagonist?
- Is there a scene showing the Lie operating in Act I, at cost?
- Does the protagonist make choices, or do things happen to them? Count the
  scenes where they initiate. Below one-third is a passive protagonist.
- Does every supporting character want something of their own?
- Dialogue swap test: exchange two characters' lines. Does anything break?
- Name the **price**: which of the ten categories (Module 03 §5b), taken when,
  and does the protagonist own anything in that category at the opening?
- Run the six premise-level checks in Module 03 §5a against the finished
  draft. A price that became a currency during drafting is a common drift.

### Pass 3 — Tension
- Open-question audit: for each chapter, list the questions live at its end.
  Any chapter with zero is a leak.
- Outcome-type sequence: list `no,and` / `yes,but` / `yes` / `no` per scene.
  Look for runs of three identical.
- Where is the reader ahead of the protagonist? If nowhere, add it.
- Is there a clock in the second half?
- Does the protagonist pay an irreversible price before the midpoint, or are
  the stakes still purely conditional at 50%?

### Pass 4 — Scene
- Every scene: name the value and its charge at entry and exit. Any scene where
  the charge is unchanged gets cut or rewritten.
- Every scene: is the POV character's goal identifiable in the first 200 words?
- Are sequels present after major disasters and proportioned to their weight?

### Pass 5 — Prose
- Run the AI blacklist from Module 05 as a literal search. Count each item, set
  a budget, cut to it.
- Filter-verb count in close-POV scenes (*saw, heard, felt, realised, noticed,
  watched, seemed*).
- Adverb count in dialogue tags. Target: near zero.
- Sentence-length variance per page. Flat variance means flat prose.
- Emotional shorthand (pounding hearts, held breaths): replace every instance.
- Read three random pages aloud. Mark every stumble.

### Pass 6 — Continuity
- Timeline consistency: days, seasons, travel times, ages.
- Physical consistency: appearance, injuries that should still hurt, objects
  that should still be where they were left.
- Knowledge consistency: who knows what, as of when. This is the one most often
  broken and the one readers catch fastest.
- Every OPEN promise: intentional, or forgotten?

## 3. Failure modes specific to machine-written novels

Check for each explicitly. They do not announce themselves.

**Uniform texture.** Every chapter roughly the same length, the same rhythm, the
same density of description. Real books are lumpy. Introduce deliberate
variation: one very short chapter, one that is almost all dialogue, one that is
almost all interiority.

**Competence inflation.** Every character articulate, insightful, emotionally
literate, and reasonable. Give people blind spots, bad arguments, inarticulacy
about what matters most to them, and opinions they cannot defend.

**Conflict avoidance.** Characters who resolve disagreements through mutual
understanding within two exchanges. Let arguments go unresolved. Let someone be
unfair and not apologise.

**Thematic over-servicing.** Every scene touching the theme, every image
reinforcing it, the meaning available on the surface. Let some scenes be about
nothing but what happens in them.

**Resolution compulsion.** Every thread tied, every character accounted for,
every question answered. Leave something ragged.

**Drift.** Voice, distance, and tense sliding across a long book. Compare
chapter 3 with chapter 33 side by side. They should sound like the same book.

**Escalation without accumulation.** Each event bigger than the last, but nothing
carrying forward. Injuries that heal by the next chapter, betrayals that produce
no lasting mistrust. Consequences must persist.

**Premise abandonment.** By chapter 20, the book is no longer about what chapter
1 promised. Reread chapter 1 against chapter 20 and check.

---

## 4. The reader simulation

The most useful single check. Read the draft as a reader who paid for it, and at
the end of each chapter answer:

1. What do I want to know right now?
2. Why am I turning this page?
3. If I put the book down here, would I pick it up again?

Any chapter where the answer to (3) is "probably not" is the real problem,
regardless of what the other checklists say.

---

## 5. Before declaring the book finished

- [ ] Passes 1–6 complete
- [ ] Every entry in PROMISES is closed or deliberately open
- [ ] Chapter 1 and the final chapter read as the same book
- [ ] The opening image and closing image are in dialogue
- [ ] The obligatory scenes for the genre are all present and dramatized
- [ ] Word count is inside the genre band
- [ ] A random page from each quarter passes the "what does the POV character
      want on this page" test
- [ ] Nothing on the AI blacklist survives above budget
- [ ] The book could not have been written about anyone else
